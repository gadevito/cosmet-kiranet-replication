# Living with an LLM Measurer — Replication Package

Companion to *Living with an LLM Measurer: Review, Verification, and Adoption Costs in Industrial COSMIC Measurement* (Kiranet, Nov 2025 – Jun 2026; CosMet re-instantiated on gpt-oss-20B,
served on-premises).

## What can and cannot be replicated

The use-case documents are confidential (they are the reason the model runs
on-premises), so the package supports replication of the **process** and of
the **analysis**, not of the **data**:

| Released | Withheld |
|----------|----------|
| Analysis code (sequence and text metrics with their exact settings, cluster bootstrap, economics, tables) | Use-case text and the element labels (only their scores are released) |
| Derived per-measurement scores, all 1,000 measurements × 10 runs, numeric only, flagged by reference type | Company few-shot examples |
| Exact inference configuration: launch command, request parameters, versions, hardware | Raw model outputs (contain requirement text) |
| Prompt skeletons (the two system prompts with the example blocks replaced by placeholders; see `prompts/README.md`) | Interview transcripts, individual questionnaire answers |
| Calibration log (20 configuration runs on the 32-use-case / 190 CFP calibration set: examples × reasoning effort × max_tokens, aggregate scores only) | Calibration use cases |
| Spot-check log (52 rows; 2 measurements with one surviving mislabelled DM each), effort ledger, setup effort, Phase 2 usage log (200 measurements: document, date, week, measurer, session) | — |
| Questionnaire (Sec. VIII-D), interview guide, codebook (Sec. VIII-A–C: 12 inductive codes organised under the three RQ2 areas, participant-level 0/1 counts, quotations) | — |

`bash run_all.sh` regenerates from `data/` the paper's tables (Table I agreement, Table II workloads/
throughput, Table III usage, codebook counts), the agreement results, the two sensitivity analyses, the
operational-draft check, the run-to-run stability, the usage aggregates, throughput and reductions from the
recorded person-days, the adoption ledger and the economic scenario, and the questionnaire statistics
(see *Reproduced figures* below).

## Three levels of reproducibility

1. **Recomputable from the released data**: every accuracy, agreement, repeatability, sensitivity and
   operational-draft figure (`scores_per_measurement.csv`); usage aggregates (`usage_log.csv`); throughput,
   reductions, ledger and scenario (`effort_ledger.csv`); spot-check counts and minutes (`spot_checks.csv`);
   codebook counts (`codebook.csv`); calibration grids (`calibration_log.csv`).
2. **Documented but not re-executable here, for confidentiality**: running CosMet itself (confidential
   few-shot examples and use cases), the text-similarity scoring of the Italian labels (procedure and
   settings in `text_metrics.py`; only the resulting scores are released), the interviews (guide released,
   transcripts withheld), the questionnaire (instrument released, individual answers withheld; the per-item
   statistics are copied in `qualitative/questionnaire.md`).
3. **Retrospective estimates or company information**: the setup effort of Sec. V-B (24–40 h: retrospective
   estimates covering all contributors, not timesheet records, excluding parallel-run validation, adjudication,
   spot-checks and routine maintenance; hardware already available), the company share of setup (1.0 pd) and
   the Phase 1 adjudication (≈1 pd), the loaded daily rate (EUR 240), the indicative workstation price.

## How the Phase 1 references were settled

The academic authors ran CosMet on the Phase 1 material and prepared, for each measurement whose
draft differed from the manual analysis, a one-page comparison (manual sheet vs. CosMet's step-by-step
analysis). The decision was the company's: the original measurer and the company's senior measurer
(five years of COSMIC experience) examined each difference in the company's routine review sessions
and decided it under the COSMIC rules; an academic author attended to answer questions about the
draft and had no vote. Outcomes: (a) manual analysis confirmed (the large majority: the draft's
difference is scored as an error); (b) manual analysis corrected (11 measurements, flagged by
`reference_corrected_in_phase1_review`); (c) unresolved ambiguity: the initial manual measurement is
retained. Company time for these sessions was about 1 person-day in total for both people together
(four sessions of roughly one hour each, two people, Jan-Mar 2026: 8 person-hours for the 110 Phase 1
discrepancies, about 4 minutes per discrepancy with the comparison sheets prepared in advance); it was
booked on the `COLLAB` timesheet code, not on the `FSM` measurement codes. It is a separate row of
`effort_ledger.csv` (`phase = adjudication`, 8 h = 1.0 pd): an adoption cost, excluded from the manual
baseline and from routine throughput. It is part of the paper's company total: 32.0 + 2.04 + 2.3 + 1.0 (adjudication) + 1.0 (setup share)
= 38.3 pd, reported as approximately 38 (adjudication and the setup share are estimates, so no more precision
is claimed). This changes neither the steady-state scenario nor
the conclusion; academic support and its setup share remain outside the company total.

## Three versions of each Phase 2 analysis (A / B / C) and what the review changed

| Version | Content | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A | operational draft actually received by the measurer | `scores_per_measurement.csv`, `operational_draft = 1` (also `spot_checks.raw_draft_sequence` for the 52) |
| B | analysis after the routine review, before any reconciliation | 148 non-checked: the accepted analysis (`ref_steps`, `ref_sequence`); 52 checked: `spot_checks.reviewed_sequence` |
| C | accepted analysis after reconciliation with the independent spot-check | 52 checked: `spot_checks.manual_sequence` |

`analysis/review_changes.py` compares A → B for the 200 Phase 2 measurements and B → C for the 52, at the
level the released data allow: movement types per use-case step and CFP total (output
`results/review_changes_phase2.csv`, one row per measurement; a re-measured process is a separate row).
Result, for the 200 measurements: step-level movement-type sequence unchanged in 175, changed in 25
(movements added or removed: 24, reclassified: 2; CFP total changed: 16; P2 8/50, P3 3/45, P4 7/55, P5 7/50).
The script also checks CFP consistency: `reviewed_cfp == accepted_cfp` in all 200 rows, so the 16 CFP changes
are the 16 operational drafts whose total differs from the reference, i.e. the 92.0% exact-count rate and the
MAE of 0.080 CFP of Sec. VII-A (same selection `operational_draft = 1`, same measurement key including
`process_version`). For the 52 spot-checked measurements the type sequence of A equals B in all 52, and B → C
differs in 2 (the two type errors, CFP unchanged): both were present in the draft and remained after the
review; none was introduced by the review.

Perimeter: these are observed changes to movement types and CFP totals only. A measurement with an unchanged
type sequence may still have required changes to data-group, object-of-interest, functional-user or
triggering-event labels, which are not in the released data; the package therefore makes no claim such as
"accepted without changes", "no review needed" or "terminology-only edits". For the 52 spot-checked
measurements, errors corrected or remaining can additionally be described on the basis of the independent
check; for the other 148 only the observed type/CFP changes can.

## Spot-check comparison criterion (the "50 of 52 agreed" of Sec. VII-A; `data/spot_checks.csv`)

* What was compared: the checker's independent manual analysis against the measurer's reviewed
  analysis, step by step on the use case's numbered steps. Elements checked per step: the data
  movements present, their type (E/X/R/W) and their position in the step, and for each movement the
  identified data group and object of interest; at process level the functional user and the
  triggering event. Not just the CFP total: the two discrepancies left the CFP total unchanged.
* Discrepancy = a difference in the COSMIC analysis: a movement present in one analysis and absent in
  the other, a different type, or a different *concept* identified as data group / object of interest
  (e.g. a different source for a Read). Columns `first_comparison_discrepancy` (outcome of the first comparison), `discrepant_dm`, `discrepancy_description` (the two cases: Exit instead of Entry for readings sent by a device; Entry instead of Read for a code taken from a persistent configuration table; CFP unchanged in both), `accepted_value_corrected`, `review_minutes`.
* Terminology: two labels judged by the two measurers to denote the same data group or object of
  interest (e.g. "Prenotazione" vs "Testata prenotazione", singular/plural, abbreviations) are NOT a
  discrepancy; they were noted on the spot-check form and are counted in
  `label_wording_differences` (per measurement, 0–2). This is expert judgement and is deliberately
  looser than the automatic joint agreement of `step_agreement.py`, which requires identical
  normalised strings; the two figures are therefore not comparable and the paper does not compare them.
* When: the comparison was done once, after both analyses were complete, with no correction of either
  before it. "Agreed" = no discrepancy at that first comparison (50 of 52). For the 2 discrepant
  measurements the accepted value was corrected afterwards (`accepted_value_corrected`).

## Familiarity with previously measured processes (a confound of the manual-vs-assisted comparison)

* All 400 maintenance measurements (Phases 1–2, P2–P5) concern revised versions of processes that
  already had an accepted value in the product baseline (the products have been measured since 2024);
  the measurer always measures the complete updated process (Sec. VI-B). Prior acquaintance with the
  process is therefore a condition of maintenance measurement in both phases, not a Phase 2 peculiarity.
* Within the period, the ≈60 measurements of processes already measured once between January and June
  2026 (Sec. VI-B) are all in Phase 2: 0 in Phase 1, 60 of 200 in Phase 2 (P2 20/50, P3 11/45, P4 18/55,
  P5 11/50; 53 second and 7 third measurements). Their share per product does not line up with the
  effort reduction (P2 40% → 72%, P3 24% → 60%, P4 33% → 85%, P5 22% → 78%), but effort is booked per
  session, not per measurement, so the contribution of familiarity to the Phase 2 reduction cannot be
  isolated; the comparison is of distinct workloads (Table II caption), not of paired observations.
* Assignment: each product has a primary measurer (usage log: P2→M1, P3→M3, P4→M2, P5→M4, 70–85% of each
  product's Phase 2 measurements); the same product ownership applied in Phases 0–1, so the updated
  process was normally measured by the person who had measured the previous version. Phase 2
  assignment is recorded per measurement (`usage_log.csv`, `measurer`); for Phase 1 it is not released
  per measurement and the statement describes the company's practice.
* Access to the previous measurement: the baseline measurement sheet of the previous version is in the
  company repository and available to the measurer in both phases; consulting it is customary and
  optional, not prescribed. CosMet never receives it (Sec. VI-B: no access to earlier versions or change
  annotations).

## Retrospective independent check of the routine review (`data/retrospective_check_sample.csv`, `data/retrospective_check.csv`, October 2026)

The review-change figures (`analysis/review_changes.py`) document review activity, not review effectiveness:
the 25 measurements whose sequence the review changed all lie outside the 52 spot-checked ones. To establish
whether those edits were correct, a sample of 50 of the 148 non-checked Phase 2 measurements was drawn for an
independent re-measurement after the study period: the 25 review-changed ones and 25 drawn at random
(numpy `default_rng(20260629).choice`, without replacement, over the candidate ids in file order; the script re-runs the draw and checks that it reproduces the sample) from the 122 unchanged ones with a retrievable document version. The sample lists only
identifiers and the step count of the measured version (from the company's own extraction, used as a
version check); the checker measures blind to the draft and to the accepted sheet. `analysis/retrospective_check.py`
classifies each returned measurement against the draft (A) and the accepted sheet (B): for the 25 review-changed
ones, *confirmed* (B = C ≠ A), *reversed* (A = C ≠ B) or *partial* (C differs from both); for the other 25,
*agreed* (B = C) or *residual* (B ≠ C). The check was performed in October 2026 by one measurer (checker `CHK`
in the returned sheet) on all 50 measurements (none unretrievable); `results/retrospective_check.csv` holds the
three sequences and the outcome per measurement.

| Group | N | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence changed by the review | 25 | 23 confirmed, 2 partial, 0 reversed |
| Sequence unchanged by the review | 25 | 24 agreed, 1 residual |

In both *partial* cases the checker's notes confirm the movement introduced by the review and differ from the
accepted sheet on another movement (M0695: closing Exit not counted, CFP 6 vs 7; M0967: Read instead of Entry
at the last step, CFP unchanged); the *residual* case is an Exit instead of Read at the last step (M0964, CFP
unchanged). The checker's CFP equals the accepted CFP in 49/50 and the draft CFP in 35/50. Together with the
52 spot checks of the study period, 77 of the 200 Phase 2 measurements were independently re-measured; five
step-level classification discrepancies survived the routine review, one of which changes the CFP total by 1.

## How effort was recorded (`data/effort_ledger.csv`, `data/spot_checks.csv`, `data/usage_log.csv`)

Personnel effort was recorded in the company timesheet system at session level, in increments of
0.01 eight-hour person-days. Each entry identified a measurer, date, and product/release activity
code. Ledger values are sums of these entries by project and phase. Hour equivalents are derived by
multiplying person-days by eight. Throughput and effort reductions are calculated from the summed
recorded person-days, without rounding intermediate quantities.

Timesheet codes (column `timesheet_code` of the ledger):

| Code | What is booked on it | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `FSM` | routine measurement work, manual (Phases 0–1) or assisted (Phase 2): one code per product/release; includes the routine review by a second measurer where the company's practice requires it | manual baseline (32.0 pd) and assisted effort (2.04 pd); Table II throughput |
| `FSM-check` | the 52 independent manual spot-checks of Phase 2, booked by the checker; includes the two reconciliation discussions | 2.3 pd, study cost, excluded from throughput |
| `COLLAB` | company time on the collaboration outside measurement: setup share (1.0 pd, Sec. V-B) and Phase 1 discrepancy adjudication (1.0 pd) | adoption costs; excluded from the manual baseline and from throughput; both are in the paper's ≈38 pd company total |
| not booked by the company | academic authors' Phase 1 validation (13 pd) and their setup share (Sec. V-B, estimates) | reported separately, outside the company total |

* Session entries: one per measurer per day per activity code; the Phase 2 `FSM` entries (69) are
  released in `usage_log.csv`, column `session_person_days`; for Phases 0–1 only the sums are released.
  Entries are made at the end of the working session (same day); no per-process timer.
* Unit: the session (one measurer, one day, one product). Time is not apportioned to documents or
  functional processes; throughput (CFP per person-day) is therefore reported per project and
  phase only (Table II / `tab:disagg`), never per document or process.
* Scope of a session entry: hands-on measurement work only (reading the requirement, running CosMet,
  reviewing and correcting the draft, validating); breaks and other tasks are excluded as in normal
  timesheet practice. Inference wait time (2–5 s per process, Sec. VII-B) falls inside the session and
  is included; batch runs of a whole document (~1–2 min) were not subtracted.
* People: one measurer per document, plus the company's routine review by a second measurer where its
  review practice requires it (larger documents before release sign-off); the second measurer logs that
  time under the same `FSM` code, so it is inside the manual and the assisted figures alike. Spot-checks are
  logged by the checker under `FSM-check` (2.3 pd = 18.4 h = 1,104 min, the sum of `review_minutes`
  in `spot_checks.csv`, which the checker noted on the spot-check form); the two reconciliation
  discussions are inside that figure. Phase 1 comparison work was done by the academic authors on
  their own time (13 pd, `who = academic authors`, not in the company ledger).
* Same timesheet, codes and granularity in Phase 0, 1 and 2.
* Order of computation (`analysis/make_tables.py`, `analysis/economics.py`): throughput =
  CFP / person_days_actual; reduction = 1 − 150 / throughput, where 150 = 4,800 / 32.0 is the pooled
  manual throughput; the assisted total first sums CFP (1,200) and person-days (0.57 + 0.69 + 0.33 +
  0.45 = 2.04, recorded values) and then divides: 1,200 / 2.04 = 588.2 → 588 CFP/pd, 74.5%. No sum of
  rounded throughputs and no simple mean of the four products. Rounding happens only when the table is
  formatted (person-days as recorded, throughput to the unit with Python's round-half-to-even, hence
  305 / 2.00 = 152.5 → 152 for P5 Phase 1).

## Statistical definitions (Sec. VI-D of the paper, implemented in `analysis/bootstrap_ci.py`)

* Runs are averaged **within** each measurement first; the point estimate is
  the pooled mean over measurements (each weighs 1); SD is the sample SD
  (n−1) over the per-measurement means.
* Accuracy is computed, as in Sec. VII-A, on **all 1,000 measurements / 6,000
  CFP** (852 with an independent manual reference, 148 whose reference
  descends from the reviewed draft); `--independent-only` restricts to the
  852 as a sensitivity check. The script also prints the supplementary
  descriptives of Sec. VII-A: the micro-averaged ROUGE-L recall (rounded sum
  of run-averaged LCS lengths over 6,000 reference DM), the equal-project-
  weight F1, and the exact-count rate (mean of the ten execution-level
  percentages).
* 95% CIs: **cluster bootstrap** over the five projects, B = 10,000,
  **percentile** method (no BCa). With five clusters only 126 distinct
  resamples exist, so intervals are coarse and the MdAE interval is
  degenerate; the paper says so.
* Published GPT-4 values (original study) are shown next to each CI as a
  compatibility check only — different datasets, not a significance test.

## Inference configuration (Sec. V-A)

See `environment/`. In short: `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (MXFP4, revision `6cee5e8`) on vLLM 0.11.0 via
the OpenAI-compatible endpoint, `temperature 0`, `top_p 1`, penalties 0, `max_tokens 8192`,
`reasoning_effort medium`, single request at a time; RTX 5090 32 GB desktop.
`hardware.md` states precisely what "≈200 tok/s" and "≈2–5 s per functional
process" measure, and that the workstation pre-existed the adoption (hardware
is therefore excluded from the economics, Sec. VIII-E, which gives the indicative purchase cost).

## Economics (`analysis/economics.py`)

Keeps the two things the paper keeps apart: the **scenario** (6,000 CFP:
40 person-days manually vs 6–16 assisted at the 60–85% min–max Phase 2
reduction; gross EUR 5.8k–8.2k per portfolio, EUR 12k–16k/year ≈ 0.20–0.28
FTE) and the **adoption ledger** (what the company actually spent:
≈38 pd = 32.0 manual + 2.04 assisted + 2.3 spot-checks + 1.0 Phase 1 adjudication + 1.0 setup share, vs 40
manual; ~13 pd of Phase 1 validation borne by the academic authors, distinct from setup). Every rate is a flag.
